How to Sell a House in Bucks County, PA: A Step-by-Step Guide

Selling a house in Bucks County takes about 2 to 3 months from listing to settlement. In September 2026, homes spent an average of 29 days on the market, according to the Bucks County Association of REALTORS (BCAR). Settlement usually follows about 30 to 45 days after an accepted offer.

Most sellers follow the same steps: know your value, choose how to sell, prepare the home and paperwork, list, accept an offer, then settle. This guide covers each step, along with the Bucks County details national guides often miss, like the 2% transfer tax and township resale inspections.

Step 1: Know What Your Home Is Worth

Every decision in a sale starts with your home's value. It sets your list price, shows what you will walk away with, and tells you if a cash offer is fair.

Bucks County prices are holding steady. The median sale price was $515,000 in September 2026, up 1% from a year earlier, according to BCAR (see the Bucks County housing market 2026). But values swing a lot by town, school district, lot size and condition. A countywide number won't tell you what your house is worth.

Step 2: Choose How You Will Sell

You have three main paths:

  • List with a local agent. This usually gets the highest price and the most buyers. Commission is negotiable, so ask each agent what their fee covers.
  • Sell to a cash buyer. This is fast and as-is, with no showings. Offers are usually below market value.   • Sell it yourself (FSBO). You skip the listing commission, but you handle pricing, marketing, showings and paperwork.

If you choose an agent, talk to two or three. Ask how many homes they sold in your town last year, how they would price yours, and how they will market it. Compare the paths in more detail on our Sell My House in Bucks County page.

Step 3: Pick the Right Time to List

Spring and early summer bring the most buyers. Fall brings serious buyers and less competition from other sellers. Winter has fewer buyers, but homes that are priced sharply still sell.

Right now, pricing matters more than timing. Buyers have more choice than earlier this year. BCAR reported 1,062 active listings in September 2026, up from 591 in January. More choice gives buyers time to compare, so a fair price matters. Read more on whether now is a good time to sell in Bucks County.

Step 4: Gather Your Documents

Pull these together before you list. It saves days once an offer comes in:

  • Your deed and your mortgage payoff details
  • Recent property tax and utility bills
  • HOA or condo documents, if you have them
  • Permits and receipts for work done on the home
  • Warranties for the roof, HVAC, appliances or other systems

Step 5: Fill Out the Pennsylvania Seller Disclosure

Pennsylvania law requires sellers to tell buyers about known material defects in the home. You do this on a signed seller disclosure statement. The buyer must get it before the agreement of sale is signed.

The form asks about the roof, basement water, heating, plumbing, electrical, the septic system and more. Answer honestly. Leaving out a known problem can lead to a lawsuit after settlement.

Some sales are exempt. These include sales by an executor, administrator, trustee or guardian, foreclosure sales, and transfers between co-owners or to a spouse or close relative. As-is and cash sales are not exempt. If anything changes before settlement, you must tell the buyer.

Step 6: Check Your Township's Resale U&O Rules

Many Bucks County townships and boroughs require a resale use and occupancy (U&O) inspection before a home can change hands. Bensalem, Lower Southampton and Warwick all require one, for example. Newtown Township does not require one for residential resales.

Township Fee When to apply
Bensalem $100 One month before settlement. Needs electrical, heating and chimney certificates
Lower Southampton $125, reinspection $75 At least 30 days before settlement. Includes a sewer lateral video
Warwick $200, first reinspection included At least 30 days before settlement

Fees are from each township's current resale U&O form. Check with your township before you apply.

Rules differ by township. Call your township office as soon as you decide to sell, and apply at least 30 days before settlement. If the inspector finds problems, you can fix them before settlement. Under Pennsylvania's Act 133, a buyer can also accept a temporary certificate and fix them within 12 months. If the problems make the home unsafe to live in, the buyer gets a temporary access certificate instead and can't move in until the repairs are done.

Step 7: Prepare Your Home for Buyers

You don't need a full renovation. Focus on what buyers and inspectors notice:

  • Fix known problems, like roof leaks, electrical issues and an old water heater.
  • Service the furnace and air conditioning, and keep the receipts.
  • Deep clean and clear out clutter, closets included.
  • Paint scuffed or bold walls a neutral color.
  • Clean up the yard, the front door and the walkway.

Step 8: Price It Right From Day One

Price is the biggest factor in how fast you sell. In September 2026, Bucks County homes spent an average of 29 days on the market (BCAR). Homes priced above recent sales often sit longer, and they may sell for less after price cuts.

Price in line with recent sales near you. Use sales from the last 60 to 90 days, because the market has changed during 2026. A fair price from day one draws more buyers.

Step 9: Review Offers and Sign the Agreement of Sale

The highest price is not always the strongest offer. Compare these points:

  • Financing. Cash, conventional, FHA or VA, and the size of the down payment.
  • Contingencies. Inspection, mortgage, the lender's value check, or the sale of the buyer's current home.
  • Earnest money. A bigger deposit shows a more committed buyer.
  • Settlement date. Does it fit your move?

If an offer is above list price, ask how the buyer will cover a gap if the lender's value comes in low. A lender only lends on that value, so a low figure can reopen the price talks.

Step 10: Get Through the Inspection and the Lender's Value Check

Most buyers order a home inspection soon after the agreement is signed. If it finds problems, the buyer may ask for repairs, a credit at settlement or a lower price. You can say yes, say no or counter.

If the buyer has a mortgage, the lender orders its own value check. If it comes in below the price, you and the buyer agree on a new price, the buyer covers the difference, or the deal can end under that contingency.

Step 11: Know Your Selling Costs in Bucks County

Your main costs are your agent's commission, your share of the transfer tax and any repair credits you agree to. Commission is negotiable. On a $515,000 home, the full 2% transfer tax is $10,300. If you split it 50/50 with the buyer, your share is $5,150.

Step 12: Settlement

In Pennsylvania, settlement is usually handled by a title company. You sign the deed, your mortgage is paid off from the proceeds, and the transfer tax is paid when the deed is recorded. Then you hand over the keys.

Bring a photo ID, all house keys, garage door openers and any alarm codes. Schedule final meter readings and cancel your utilities for the day after settlement.

Selling in a Special Situation

Inherited house. The executor needs Letters Testamentary (or Letters of Administration) from the Bucks County Register of Wills before selling. Executors don't have to fill out the seller disclosure.

Divorce. Every owner on the deed must sign. Agree on the price and how you will split the proceeds before you list.

Relocating on a deadline. See our guide to selling your Bucks County home fast.

The house needs major work. You can sell as-is to a cash buyer or list as-is with an agent. Know your value first so you can compare both.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to sell a house in Bucks County?

About 2 to 3 months from listing to settlement. Homes spent an average of 29 days on the market in September 2026 (BCAR). Settlement usually takes about 30 to 45 days after an accepted offer.

How much does it cost to sell a house in Bucks County?

It depends mostly on your agent's commission, which is negotiable, and your share of the 2% transfer tax. Your mortgage payoff comes out of the proceeds too.

Who pays the transfer tax in Bucks County?

It is set in the agreement of sale and often split 50/50. That split is a custom, not a law. By law, the buyer and seller are both responsible for the full tax if it isn't paid. The total is 2% of the property's value.

Do I need a lawyer to sell a house in Pennsylvania?

No. A title company usually handles settlement. Some sellers hire a real estate attorney for an estate sale, a divorce or a sale without an agent.

Can I sell my house if I still owe on the mortgage?

Yes. Your loan is paid off from the sale proceeds at settlement. You receive what is left after the payoff and your closing costs.
