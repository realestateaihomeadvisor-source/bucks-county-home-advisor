How Do Online Home Value Estimators Actually Work

How estimators work · September 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Online home value estimators, called AVMs, calculate your home’s value using an algorithm trained on public records, tax assessments, and recent comparable sales in your area. They cannot see inside your home, which is why national tools like Zillow’s Zestimate carry a median error of just 1.74% for listed homes, but jump to around 7.2% for homes not yet on the market. Local estimators trained on regional data typically perform more consistently for a specific county market.

Here’s how home value estimators work behind the number.

The basic mechanics of an AVM

An automated valuation model, or AVM, pulls three main types of data: public property records, county tax assessments, and recent sale prices of comparable homes nearby.

What the model uses What it does with it
Public property records Size, age, and location of your home
County tax assessments A public record baseline the model can compare
Recent comparable sales Nearby sale prices, adjusted for differences

The algorithm compares your home’s size, age, and location against those recent sales, then adjusts for differences to land on an estimated value.

Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com, and most bank valuation tools all use this same basic approach, even though each builds its own proprietary model with slightly different weighting.

Why the same house gets different numbers on different sites

Since every AVM draws from largely the same underlying data, you’d expect similar results. In practice, they often diverge by tens of thousands of dollars.

The difference comes down to how each model weighs recent sales, how often it updates, and how it handles homes with limited nearby comparables.

This is also why on-market and off-market estimates differ so much. A home currently listed gives the algorithm a real asking price to anchor against. A home not yet listed relies entirely on older comparable sales, which is inherently less precise.

What an AVM can’t see

The single biggest limitation of any automated estimate is that it has never been inside your home.

It doesn’t know about your renovated kitchen, your finished basement, your new roof, or the fact that your backyard backs onto a busy road. All of these move real value up or down, sometimes significantly.

This limitation matters more for older, custom, or unique homes, and less for newer subdivisions where nearby homes share similar floor plans and features.

Why local data produces more reliable results

A nationwide AVM is built to work reasonably well everywhere, which means it isn’t finely tuned to any single county’s pricing patterns.

A Bucks County home value estimator built specifically on local sales data can better reflect township-level trends, since it isn’t averaging across markets that behave completely differently from Doylestown or Newtown.

This matters even more right now, with Bucks County prices moving faster than the national average in several townships.

How to use an estimate the right way

Treat any online estimate as a starting point, not a final number. Use it to get a general sense of where your home sits before doing anything else.

Then compare it against a handful of recent sales in your specific neighborhood, since township-level and even street-level differences can be significant.

If you’re seriously considering selling, follow up with a local walkthrough. Frequently asked questions

Are online home value estimates reliable?

They’re a reasonable starting point, especially for homes currently listed, but they shouldn’t replace a local comp analysis or in-person walkthrough before pricing a home. Estimates on this site are AI-generated using public market data.

Why does my home’s estimate change over time?

AVMs update as new sales data becomes available in your area, so values naturally shift as comparable homes sell nearby.

Do estimators work better in some neighborhoods than others?

Yes. They tend to be more accurate in areas with frequent, similar home sales, and less accurate for older, custom, or unique properties with fewer comparables.

Is a local estimator more accurate than Zillow or Redfin?

For county-specific markets, a local tool built on regional sales data can better reflect area-specific trends than a nationwide model averaging across very different markets.
