Renovation ROI in 2026: Which Upgrades Actually Pay Off in PA

Renovation ROI · September 29, 2026 · 6 min read

In 2026, exterior projects deliver the highest ROI by far. Garage door replacement averages 268%, steel entry doors around 188–216%, and manufactured stone veneer over 208%. Inside the home, minor kitchen remodels return about 96–113%, while midrange bathroom updates return 74–80%. Major remodels, additions, and pools all underperform, often recovering less than half their cost.

Here’s the full 2026 ranking, and what it means for PA homeowners specifically.

Why exterior projects dominate the rankings

Nine of the top ten highest-ROI projects nationally are exterior improvements, according to the latest annual Cost vs. Value Report.

The reasoning is straightforward. Buyers judge a home’s condition before they ever step inside, and exterior projects cost far less than interior remodels while delivering outsized visual impact.

Garage door replacement has held the top spot for six of the last seven years. A typical project costs around $4,672 and returns roughly $12,507 in added value.

Steel entry door replacement follows closely, recovering 75% to over 200% of its cost depending on the market, alongside better energy efficiency and security.

Interior ROI: kitchens beat bathrooms, but scope matters more

On raw percentage, a minor kitchen remodel outperforms a midrange bathroom remodel, roughly 96–113% versus 74–80%.

But the gap closes fast once you increase scope. A major kitchen remodel above $80,000 drops to around 40–60% ROI, while an upscale bathroom addition falls to around 42–45%.

The pattern repeats across nearly every category in the 2026 data. The smaller and more targeted the project, the closer it comes to paying for itself. The larger and more personalized, the further it falls behind.

The accessibility shift worth watching

One trend growing fast in the 2026 data is aging-in-place renovation. Universal-design bathroom updates — grab bars, wider doorways, curbless showers — gained 12 ROI points in a single year, now recovering around 61%.

This tracks with demographics. A large majority of adults over 50 want to stay in their current home rather than move, and most expect to add accessibility features eventually.

For PA sellers with an older buyer pool in their target market, this is a category worth watching, since it appeals to a broader range of buyers than a purely aesthetic upgrade.

What consistently underperforms

Based on the 2026 data, these categories deliver the weakest returns and are best avoided if resale is the primary goal:

  • Swimming pools: 24–50% ROI, and can actively hurt value in colder PA winters
  • High-end home office conversions: around 28% ROI
  • Major upscale kitchen remodels: 40–60% ROI
  • Upscale primary bath additions: 42–45% ROI

These numbers are national averages, not guarantees. Your actual return depends on your home’s price tier, your specific PA market, and whether you’re selling into a buyer’s or seller’s market.

Applying this to your Bucks County home

Given current market conditions, where inventory is tight and homes are still selling quickly, you may not need extensive renovation to get a strong offer.

The smarter approach for most PA sellers is targeted spending: fix visible exterior wear, do a minor kitchen or bathroom refresh if genuinely dated, and skip large-scope projects entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Which renovation has the highest ROI in 2026?

Garage door replacement, averaging 268% nationally, the highest of any tracked home improvement project.

Do kitchen or bathroom remodels return more value?

On percentage terms, minor kitchen remodels edge out bathroom remodels, but bathrooms can matter more if a home has multiple outdated bathrooms dragging down its overall condition.

Is a swimming pool worth adding before selling in PA?

Generally no. Pools return only 24–50% ROI nationally and can actively reduce value in colder northern climates like Pennsylvania.
